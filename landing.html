What this website gives you

One place for the department's academic records, for every role.

Students

  • Every course of your semester, with class tests, attendance and CATM worked out for you
  • Your GPA for each semester and your running CGPA
  • The full history of any mark: who entered it, when, and every change with its reason
  • Raise an objection on a mark you think is wrong
  • Your timetable, a private file locker for each course, and your projects

Teachers

  • Take attendance for each class in one grid
  • Enter class-test marks; see each student's CATM and a class chart
  • Correct a mark only with a reason, which the student can read

Office

  • Keep the register: batches and the students in them
  • Approve which teacher takes which class
  • Check the whole record for changes at any time

Examiners

  • Enter final exam marks
  • Publish results; GPA and semester standing follow from them

How changes are caught: every entry is saved with a fingerprint linked to the entry before it. If anyone changes a record outside this website, the check shows exactly which record was changed, and copies kept on other computers still hold the original.

Questions

How do I see my marks?

Register once with your university email, then sign in. Your home page shows every semester; open one to see each course, its class tests and your attendance.

What if a mark looks wrong?

Open that mark's history and raise an objection. Your teacher and the office are emailed, and the objection stays in the mark's history permanently — even after it is answered.

Who can see my marks?

You, the teacher of that course, and the academic office. Other students can't, and teachers can't see courses they don't teach.

How is my CATM worked out?

For a 3-credit theory course it is 21 marks: 7.5 for attendance on the department's fixed scale, and 13.5 from your best class tests — the best 3 of 4 for a 3-credit course.

Why does it ask for my parents' names?

To check you are the person the roll number belongs to. They're compared with the office's own record, in English or Bangla, and never shown to anyone else.

Password reset isn't built yet. For now, ask the academic office.
